\chapter{Trajectory pre-processing}
\label{ch:preproc}

The pre-processing turns each IGC file into a trajectory fit for analysis. This
chapter describes its steps in the order they run, one section per step. Some steps
delete single fixes; others judge the whole flight and keep or drop it.

\section{Reading the fixes}
\label{sec:parsing}

Each \texttt{B} record gives a fix $(t_i,\varphi_i,\lambda_i)$, with latitude and
longitude in degrees, together with the validity flag and the two altitudes
$h_i^{\mathrm{baro}}$ and $h_i^{\mathrm{GNSS}}$. A record that does not follow the
fixed layout of the format, or whose time of day or position is impossible, is
discarded. A missing or unreadable altitude,
including the zero the format uses for a missing reading~\cite{fai_igc_spec}, is marked as missing, and
the fix keeps its time and position.

Time is measured in seconds from the first fix. Records carry only the time of day,
so a flight crossing UTC midnight would jump back by a day; a day is added when the
clock passes from the last hour of one day to the first hour of the next. Other
backward steps and repeated times are left to the clock rule of
Section~\ref{sec:clock}. Well-formed but implausible fixes, such as position spikes,
are kept as well: the parser discards only what cannot be read. The validity flag, which marks
fixes without a three-dimensional GNSS solution, is kept but not used at this stage.

\section{Choice of altitude channel}
\label{sec:altchannel}

Some recorders log no pressure altitude, so we use GNSS altitude for every flight,
$z=h^{\mathrm{GNSS}}$. Its vertical datum is not uniform across recorders: the IGC
specification prescribes height above the WGS84 ellipsoid~\cite{fai_igc_spec}, the
CIVL specification for hang gliders and paragliders height above the
geoid~\cite{fai_civl_s7h_2024}. A constant offset cancels in altitude increments, but
comparing absolute altitudes across flights would need a datum audit.

Let $N$ be the number of fixes read from the file, before any is deleted, and $P$ the
set of those with a GNSS altitude. The flight is kept only if
\begin{equation}
  \frac{|P|}{N}\geq\cfgAltitudeGnssPresentMin
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \max_{i\in P}h_i^{\mathrm{GNSS}}-\min_{i\in P}h_i^{\mathrm{GNSS}}\geq\SI{\cfgAltitudeGnssMinRangeM}{\metre}.
  \label{eq:gnss-admission}
\end{equation}
The first condition rejects incomplete channels; the second rejects channels stuck at
one value, and flights with almost no vertical excursion. Fixes flagged as lacking a
three-dimensional solution count as present if they carry an altitude. Pressure
altitude is never substituted for a missing GNSS value.

The barometer is checked with the same two conditions on $h^{\mathrm{baro}}$, with
thresholds \cfgAltitudeBaroWitnessPresentMin\ and
\SI{\cfgAltitudeBaroWitnessMinRangeM}{\metre}. A flight whose
barometer passes is marked, so that later cleaning steps can use pressure altitude as
independent evidence; the mark does not decide whether the flight is kept.

\section{Clock}
\label{sec:clock}

A recorder can write a time equal to or earlier than the one before it. On the fixes
as read, the clock rule keeps the first fix, and fix $i$ only if its time is later
than that of every fix before it,
\begin{equation}
  t_i>\max_{j<i}t_j.
  \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
Of several fixes with the same second, the first is kept. A backward step is deleted,
and so is every fix after it until the clock passes the latest time already kept. The
times left are strictly increasing, so every step between consecutive fixes has a
positive duration (Section~\ref{sec:fasttracks}).

\section{Repeated positions}
\label{sec:repeated}

Some recorders repeat their last position while the clock runs, so a fix can carry the
coordinates of the one before it without being a new measurement. On the fixes left by
the clock rule, numbered again in order, fix $i$ is deleted if
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_i=\varphi_{i-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_i=\lambda_{i-1}.
  \label{eq:repeated}
\end{equation}
The comparison is exact: the two records carry the same digits. Altitudes are not
compared. In a run of fixes at the same position only the first is kept.

\section{Fast tracks}
\label{sec:fasttracks}

A free flight spends part of its time climbing in thermals at a low ground speed, so
the typical speed of its track stays well below the top speed of the wing. A track
with a higher typical speed comes from something else, such as a powered aircraft or a
file with compressed times, and the whole flight is dropped.

Let $n$ be the number of fixes left by the two previous rules. Between fix $i$ and
fix $i+1$ the horizontal distance is taken on a sphere of radius
$R=\SI{6371}{\kilo\metre}$, with the haversine formula
\begin{equation}
  d_i=2R\arcsin\sqrt{\sin^2\frac{\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i}{2}
  +\cos\varphi_i\cos\varphi_{i+1}\sin^2\frac{\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i}{2}},
  \label{eq:haversine}
\end{equation}
with the angles in radians, and the step speed is
\begin{equation}
  v_i=\frac{d_i}{t_{i+1}-t_i},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1,
  \label{eq:step-speed}
\end{equation}.
The clock rule makes every denominator
positive. The typical speed of the flight is the median of its step speeds,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde v=\operatorname{median}\{v_1,\dots,v_{n-1}\},
  \label{eq:median-speed}
\end{equation}
and the flight is kept if $\tilde v$ does not exceed
\SI[per-mode=symbol]{\cfgSpeedMedianMaxKmhPara}{\kilo\metre\per\hour} for
paragliders and
\SI[per-mode=symbol]{\cfgSpeedMedianMaxKmhHang}{\kilo\metre\per\hour} for hang
gliders. A flight with fewer than two fixes has no step and is kept. The rule deletes
no fix, and the median is not moved by a few wrong steps, such as a position spike:
those are left to a later step.

The thresholds were read off the distribution of $\tilde v$ over all flights
(Figure~\ref{fig:median-speed}). In both disciplines the bulk of the distribution ends
below the threshold, and beyond it only a sparse tail of single flights remains.
Split by wing class (Figures~\ref{fig:median-speed-para-classes}
and~\ref{fig:median-speed-hang-classes}), the bulk of every class stays below the
threshold. The faster shoulder of the hang-glider distribution comes from the rigid
Class~2 wings.

\section{Horizontal excursions}
\label{sec:excursions}

A step faster than any glider of the discipline can fly is a recorder error. The
excursion rule uses a top speed $v_{\max}$, read off the data at the end of this section,
to delete the fixes such steps lead to, or to split the flight where the track does not
come back.

The rule scans the fixes left by the previous steps in order, against an anchor $a$, the
last fix accepted. Fix $j$ is reachable from the anchor if
\begin{equation}
  d(a,j)\leq v_{\max}\,(t_j-t_a),
  \label{eq:reachable}
\end{equation}
with $d(a,j)$ the distance of Equation~\eqref{eq:haversine} between the two fixes. A
reachable fix is accepted and becomes the anchor. An unreachable fix $j$ opens a block,
and the rule looks for a later fix $k$ reachable from the anchor within
$W=\SI{\cfgExcursionWindowS}{\second}$ of the opening, $t_k-t_j\leq W$. If there is one,
the track went out and came back: fixes $j$ to $k-1$ are deleted and $k$ becomes the
anchor. If there is none, the track does not come back: the flight is split between the
anchor and fix $j$, and a new segment starts from $j$. A block still open at the end of
the track is deleted.

The first anchor of each segment, at the start of the track or after a split, is the
first fix whose step to the next fix satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:reachable}; the fixes
before it are deleted. An anchor in the wrong place would make every good fix after it
unreachable, while a wrong fix agrees with neither of its neighbours and so never
becomes an anchor. The price is that a good fix followed by a spike is deleted with it.
The rule leaves the fixes it accepted and the places where the flight is split, which a
later step uses as the boundaries of its segments.

The step speed of Equation~\eqref{eq:step-speed} mixes real flight with recorder
errors, whose steps can be arbitrarily fast. Over a window of length $W$ the speed from
fix $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  u_i=\frac{d(i,j)}{t_j-t_i},\qquad j=\min\{k:\ t_k-t_i\geq W\},
  \label{eq:window-speed}
\end{equation}
with $d(i,j)$ as in Equation~\eqref{eq:reachable}. Across a gap the window is longer than
$W$. A single fix displaced by a distance $D$
changes $u_i$ by at most $D/W$, against $D/\Delta t$ for the two steps that touch it, so
the window speed follows real flight much further than the step speed
(Figure~\ref{fig:top-speed}, lower row). Both distributions fall steeply; the step speed
then turns into a long tail of errors, the window speed only later and much lower.

The window speed still carries errors that last longer than $W$, such as a jump that
never comes back. Split by wing class (Figure~\ref{fig:top-speed}, upper row), real
flight depends on the wing: each class falls off in the order of its performance. The
errors do not depend on the wing, and beyond the fall-off the curves of all classes
merge into one common tail. Real flight therefore ends where the fastest class reaches
that tail. We set $v_{\max}$ there for paragliders,
\SI[per-mode=symbol]{\cfgSpeedMaxKmhPara}{\kilo\metre\per\hour}, and with a margin above
the end of the rigid Class~2 curve for hang gliders,
\SI[per-mode=symbol]{\cfgSpeedMaxKmhHang}{\kilo\metre\per\hour}.

The window lasts as long as the time the excursion rule waits for a track to come
back. The rule splits a flight only where the mean speed from the last accepted fix
stays above $v_{\max}$ for $W$, so a threshold above every real window speed never
splits real flight.
